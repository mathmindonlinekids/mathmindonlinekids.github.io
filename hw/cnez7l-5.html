1. Warm-up

Look for magic pairs first: 2 × 5 = 10, 4 × 25 = 100, 8 × 125 = 1000.

2. Equal Groups

Division is the opposite of multiplication: 56 ÷ 7 = 8 because 7 × 8 = 56.

3. Divide with a Remainder

Make as many full groups as you can. What is left is the remainder (R).
The remainder is always smaller than the number you divide by.

4. Check Your Division

Check: quotient × divisor + remainder = the number we divided.

5. Remainder in a Story

Read the question carefully. Think: do we need one more group? Do we drop the remainder? Or do we say what is left?

6. Divide by Parts

Split the big number into parts that are easy to divide. Divide each part, then add. Find the parts yourself on paper!

7. Equations

① Open the brackets (multiply every part) ② put the same letters together ③ remove the number (+ or −) ④ divide ⑤ check by putting the answer back.

8. Factors and Primes

Prime number: exactly 2 factors, 1 and itself (2, 3, 5, 7…).
Composite number: more than 2 factors (4, 6, 8, 9…).
1 is neither prime nor composite.

A. Prime, composite or neither?

B. Lollipop diagrams

Fill in all the factors from smallest to largest. Each arc joins a factor pair. If a number pairs with itself, it gets a lollipop stick!

C. Factor trees

Keep splitting until every number at the bottom is prime. Find the numbers hiding behind the letters.

D. Prime factorization

Write the prime factors from smallest to largest. Make a factor tree on paper first.

9. Part and Whole

Always find one part first.
Find the part: whole ÷ bottom number = 1 part, then × top number.
Find the whole: the part ÷ top number = 1 part, then × bottom number.
